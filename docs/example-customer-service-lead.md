# Worked example — Customer Service Lead, Vietnamese native

> A complete walk-through of every output surface for a realistic
> low-band student. Use this when explaining the platform to a buyer or
> when designing new screens — it shows what the student sees, what the
> employer sees, and how the data moves between them.
>
> Numbers are illustrative but realistic for this profile. They reflect
> what the system actually produces with the current scoring rubrics.

## Role baseline (Customer Service Lead)

The customer-service lead is front-line on phone + chat with English-
speaking distributors and end customers. Handles complaints, follow-ups,
and warranty claims — speaking and listening under time pressure
dominate the day.

| Skill | Baseline | Why this bar |
|---|---|---|
| Speaking | 800 (low C1) | Live-call register; resolving an angry customer needs more than B2 vocabulary |
| Listening | 800 (low C1) | Phone audio + accent variability; missed details = wrong warranty decision |
| Writing | 700 (mid B2) | Email follow-ups must hold register but don't need C1 nuance |
| Reading intent | 750 (high B2) | Customer complaint emails frequently bury the real issue under polite hedging |
| Vocabulary | 750 (high B2) | Domain-specific business and warranty terms |
| Presenting | 650 (mid B2) | Less critical for this seat — only the monthly team report |

## Adaptive battery selection (what the selector picks for Lan)

The selector reads voice + role baseline and picks tasks where voice is
below baseline OR can't measure directly. For Lan that's all four:

| Task | Difficulty | Why selected |
|---|---|---|
| email_writing | B2 | Voice can't measure written register at all — probe directly. Voice inferred 480, baseline 700, gap 220+. |
| listen_paraphrase | B2 | Voice score 570 is well below the 800 baseline — verify under battery conditions. |
| read_summarise | B2 | Voice conflates reading with listening on the Sarah/Mark test — silent reading is the clean signal. |
| vocab_cloze | B2 | Voice score 550 is below the 750 baseline; written register is its own gap from spoken vocab. |

Total time: ~15 minutes.

## Gap-driven lesson plan (what shows on Lan's dashboard)

The plan generator produces this ranked list and consolidates duplicate
lesson types:

**1. Critical · Writing (gap 290) — Email sprint**
> Write business emails calibrated to your role — handling complaints,
> scheduling follow-ups, declining requests. Each sprint is scored on
> register, structure, and strategic content. Also covers reading
> subtext (gap 280) and business vocabulary (gap 230).
> [Start lesson →]

**2. Critical · Listening (gap 220) — Live class**
> Listening under time pressure dominates your role. We recommend a 1:1
> class with a teacher who can vary accent, pace, and difficulty —
> micro-lessons can't simulate a real customer escalation. Drills focus
> on customer-call comprehension at native pace.
> [Schedule a class →]

**3. Recommended · Speaking + Presenting (gap 180 each) — Speaking sprint**
> A 90-second monologue scored on fluency and presentation delivery.
> Topics calibrated to your role — explaining a warranty decision,
> summarising a customer call, presenting a weekly report.
> [Start lesson →]

The dashboard also shows three optional follow-ups (reading-focused
class, writing-focused class, vocab review) escalated because their
gaps exceed 200 — but they're surfaced under "Also recommended" rather
than the top 3 to avoid choice paralysis.

## Bilingual strategy

Yes, bilingual for everything the *student* reads. Strict English for
everything the *employer* reads or that's used as audit data.

| Surface | Vietnamese | English | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Task instructions ("Listen to the voicemail and type three key points") | ✅ Primary | ✅ Below | Low-band students can't follow nuanced English instructions; missing the instruction tanks the score for the wrong reason. |
| Evidence on score cards ("you wrote 'sorry for the inconvenience' three times") | ✅ Primary | ✅ Below | Cross-reading IS a learning act — student sees Vietnamese first, then the same content in English builds vocabulary. |
| Lesson plan rationale | ✅ Primary | ✅ Below | Trust-building. The student needs to understand *why* this lesson, in their first language. |
| Dashboard skill labels (Speaking, Listening…) | Tooltip on hover | ✅ Primary | Compact + the labels are CEFR-standard. |
| Score numbers, CEFR bands, gap deltas | n/a | ✅ Only | Numbers are universal; bands are CEFR-standard. |
| Task content itself (the email scenario, the audio clip, the cloze items) | ❌ | ✅ Only | The whole point is to test English. Translating the test defeats the assessment. |
| Employer dashboard | ❌ | ✅ Only | Audit-grade record; English is the lingua franca of the buyer-facing reports. |
| Internal admin tools | ❌ | ✅ Only | We maintain them. |

**Format on screen:**

> **Bằng chứng:** Bạn đã viết "sorry for the inconvenience" ba lần trong 140 từ. Câu mở đầu thiếu mạo từ và sai thì.
> *Evidence: You wrote "sorry for the inconvenience" three times in 140 words. The opening sentence missed an article and used the wrong tense.*

The Vietnamese is the primary read; the English is positioned as
deliberate cross-reading practice, not a fallback.

**Implementation pattern:** server-side translation at render time,
batched per page. The dashboard reads the student's
`students.native_language`, gathers all English evidence + plan
rationales for that page, makes one Claude call to translate them all,
and passes paired `{en, native}` data to the render components.
Translations cached in-memory per request.

## Notes on this example

- Numbers are calibrated to a real low-band profile. A higher-band
  student with similar role context would land 600-700 across the board
  and the lesson plan would shift toward speaking + listening
  refinement rather than wholesale remediation.
- Re-test deltas are the buyer-facing metric. After 6 weeks of the
  programme above, a realistic delta is +60 to +90 per skill on the
  largest-gap skills, less on the already-decent ones.
- The employer view deliberately strips the bilingual layer — the
  buyer-facing report is English-only because that's the audit
  language.
